## Why this exists

Severin has been winning more **design** projects — developing a house *with* the
client, then handing over drawings + a construction plan so the client can hire their
own builder. This is a larger, more profitable chunk of work than owner's rep / client

## Part 1 — What New York law actually allows

New York is strict, and the rules have **criminal** teeth. Governing law: Education Law
**Article 147** (Architecture); key exemptions in **§ 7307**.

### Title protection (the easy part to avoid)
- Only a NY-licensed person may use the title **"architect."**
- Using the title unlicensed = **Class A misdemeanor**. *Offering to perform*
  architectural services unlicensed = **Class E felony**. Unauthorized practice itself
  is also a Class E felony.
- Derivatives are also off-limits when they imply professional services:
  **"architectural designer," "architectural design," "architectural services,"
  "interior architecture."** Avoid all of them.

### Practice protection (the part that actually constrains us)
- NY defines the "practice of architecture" very broadly: rendering/offering services
  involving "the art, science, and aesthetics of design and construction of buildings...
  wherein the safeguarding of life, health, property, and public welfare is concerned."
  Designing a habitable building lands squarely inside this.
- NYSED: *"if you are not a licensed architect... you may not provide architectural
  services to a customer or offer to provide architectural services."*
- **Severin may still offer "design" as a service** — what he cannot offer is
  *architectural* services, and he cannot use the protected words.

### "Design-build" is a loaded term in NY — do not use it as a label
- NYSED specifically flags that when a contractor offers **"design-build,"** bundling
  design with construction "may be seen as a violation of the Education Law." That exact
  phrase is what regulators associate with unlawful bundling. Even though Severin is not
  the builder, using "design-build" invites the wrong association. **Avoid the phrase.**

### Two legitimate lanes
1. **Exempt lane — no seal required at all:**
   - Residences with **gross area ≤ 1,500 sq ft** (excluding garages, carports, porches,
     cellars, uninhabitable basements/attics). Additions OK if combined habitable total
     stays under 1,500.
   - **Farm buildings used solely for agriculture** (barns, sheds, poultry houses) —
     directly relevant to Severin's barn/agricultural specialty.
   - Minor alterations (≤ $20,000 outside NYC) not affecting structural/public safety.
   - On these he can design and produce drawings freely (still can't call it
     "architecture").
2. **Stamped lane — the main path for full custom homes (almost always > 1,500 sq ft):**
   - In NY, buildings may be designed and sealed by a licensed **Professional Engineer
     (PE)** *or* a licensed architect — both professions are authorized to prepare
     building plans. So Severin's PE partner is a legitimate design professional of
     record, not a workaround.

### The one real risk in the stamped lane: "plan stamping"
- NYSED requires the licensed professional to be the one genuinely **offering and
  responsible for** the design — not merely rubber-stamping a layout an unlicensed
  person sold and produced. (Same logic bars an interior-design firm from offering
  architectural services even if it employs an architect.)
- Practical implication: the PE must be meaningfully engaged in / responsible for the
  construction documents, and the contracts/engagement structure must reflect that.
  **This is a question for the PE and a NY licensing attorney — copy alone can't fix it.**

### Language cheat-sheet
**Avoid:** architect · architectural · architectural design / designer / services ·
"design-build" (as a label) · any phrasing that implies he *is* an architect.

**Safe to use:** building design · residential design · home design · design services ·
design consultant · building designer · concept / schematic design · design development ·
design coordination · "construction documents prepared and sealed by our licensed
professional engineer" · "a buildable, permit-ready plan you can take to the builder of
your choice."

**Strategic asset:** the "client hires their own builder" detail is a *legal positive* —
it's the opposite of the contractor-design-build pattern NYSED objects to, and it
reinforces the independent owner's-advocate brand.

## Part 2 — Positioning strategy (the business question)

Three distinct positions, not two:

1. **Pure owner's rep / consultant** (current site): "I know design and build; I'll
   advise." Legally bulletproof. But it *undersells* the design work he now does and is
   the most time-intensive, longest-duration, most job-site-bound, highest-liability
   mode.
2. **Design as a real offering:** "I design with you and deliver stamped, buildable
   drawings." Captures the larger/more profitable chunk; legally clean *if structured
   right*. But producing construction documents is **production labor** — more hours,
   more coordination, more legal overhead. Higher revenue, **not** less work.
3. **"Architect / architectural design / design-build"** — the line he cannot cross.

### Severin's actual goals (as he nears retirement)
- **Less time on the job site.**
- **Less liability.**
- Still wants a **creative** outlet.
- Wants to be **paid more for his time and work less** overall.

### Conclusion
- "Paid more + work less + lower liability" points to **premium *advisory* work** as the
  durable spine — short, high-leverage engagements that sell *judgment* (feasibility,
  "is this buildable / what will it really cost," design review, contractor selection,
  barn/structure assessment). Judgment is the asset that *appreciates* with age and is
  the lowest legal-risk work (pure advice never nears the architecture line).
- The **design push pulls against** the retirement goals: more profitable per project,
  but more hours, more overhead, more liability. So design is the higher-effort/
  higher-revenue option, **not** the wind-down path.
- **Creative outlet without the load:** Severin can satisfy the design itch by offering
  design *input* / consulting on the design process **from within an owner's-rep
  position** — shaping the design alongside the client and their architect/engineer —
  rather than taking on production of stamped drawings as the main event.

## Part 3 — Decision (2026-06-16)

- **Keep the website as-is** (owner's-rep / advisory framing) for now. It is both the
  legally safest framing and the best match for the retirement direction (less job-site
  time, less liability).
- Design work stays **opportunistic / opt-in**, offered in person — not a rebrand of the
  business and not a headline on the site.
- Revisit only if Severin decides he wants design to become a core, advertised offering.
  If so: confirm engagement structure with the PE, then have a NY licensing attorney
  review any public wording, and use the language cheat-sheet above.
