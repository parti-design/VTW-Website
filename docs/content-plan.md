# Content / SEO Plan — Article Series

> Internal working doc. `docs/` is excluded from the Jekyll build. Date: 2026-06-16.
> Purpose: draw in the affluent NYC buyer/renovator via search, build trust on the site,
> and funnel them to the Pre-Purchase Assessment / owner's-rep conversation.

## Strategy in one line
Answer the exact questions Severin's target client Googles *before* they buy or build —
with Hudson Valley specificity — so the site ranks locally, earns trust, and converts a
mention from a referral partner into a phone call. Every article ends with a soft CTA
(the Pre-Purchase Assessment or "talk to Severin").

## How to make these rank and convert (SEO mechanics)
- **Be local and specific.** "Hudson Valley," county and town names (Dutchess, Columbia,
  Ulster, Rhinebeck, Hudson, Millbrook, Kingston, Woodstock), "old farmhouse," "barn."
  Local + niche beats broad.
- **Topic clusters.** Group articles under a few pillar pages and cross-link them
  (e.g., the owner's-rep pillar links to every "managing a project" article). Internal
  links are what build topical authority for a small site.
- **Question-style headings** (H2s phrased as the questions people search) win featured
  snippets and voice search.
- **One soft CTA per article**, never a hard sell — the value-first tone *is* the
  marketing for a trust service.
- **Cadence:** continue the existing ~6-week rhythm (1 article every 4–6 weeks). Quality
  over volume; this is a wind-down-friendly engine.

## The series — organized in 5 content pillars

### Pillar A — Buying & Pre-Purchase (highest commercial intent; funnels the Assessment)
- **A1. "Is That Hudson Valley Barn Worth Saving? A Builder's Pre-Purchase Checklist"**
  - Intent: someone about to buy a property with an old barn. His moat.
  - Angle: what to look at — sill rot, frame condition, foundation, roof — and when a
    barn is a treasure vs. a money pit. Links to the buying-an-old-house checklist.
  - CTA: book a pre-purchase look before you make the offer.
- **A2. "Buying Land to Build On in the Hudson Valley: Is the Site Actually Buildable?"**
  - Intent: buyers eyeing raw land for new construction.
  - Angle: septic/perc, slope, ledge, water, wetlands, access, hidden site costs that
    blow up budgets.
- **A3. "The Pre-Purchase Property Assessment: What It Is and Why It Pays for Itself"**
  - Intent: people comparing "should I just get a home inspection?"
  - Angle: names the entry product; how a builder's assessment differs from a standard
    inspection (cost forecasting, buildability, negotiation leverage).

### Pillar B — Owner's Rep & Managing a Project (category capture + the NYC segment)
- **B1. "What Is an Owner's Representative? A Plain-English Guide for Hudson Valley Homeowners"** *(pillar page)*
  - Intent: capture the category search term; educate a market that doesn't know it.
  - Angle: what a rep does, what they don't, how they differ from a GC and an architect,
    when it's worth it. Cross-links to the whole cluster.
- **B2. "Building or Renovating from the City: How to Run a Hudson Valley Project When You Live in NYC"**
  - Intent: THE core segment — remote weekend-home owners. High value.
  - Angle: the remote-owner problem (can't be on site, don't know local trades), and how
    a local advocate solves it. "We're local, even when you're not."
- **B3. "Why Renovations Go Over Budget — and the Questions to Ask Before You Sign"**
  - Angle: builds on the change-orders post; contingency, allowances, scope gaps.

### Pillar C — Renovation & New Construction (existing site pillars)
- **C1. "New Construction in the Hudson Valley: The Early Decisions That Make or Break Your Budget"**
  - Fills the new-construction gap; the highest-leverage early choices.
- **C2. "Getting a Design That's Actually Buildable: How to Work With Your Architect"**
  - Angle: constructability + cost review, coordinating the design with construction
    reality. **Licensing care:** frame Severin as reviewing/coordinating *with* the
    client's architect/engineer for buildability and cost — never as providing
    architectural design. (See `design-positioning-research.md`.)

### Pillar D — Historic Restoration & Barns (his moat; authority + the preservation crowd)
- **D1. "Restoring a Hudson Valley Timber-Frame Barn: What's Really Involved"**
  - Authority piece; the work, the craft, the pitfalls. Shareable in the preservation
    community.
- **D2. "Caring for an Old Farmhouse: A Hudson Valley Owner's Maintenance Guide"**
  - Evergreen engagement; affluent old-home owners; builds the email/return audience.
- **D3. "Historic Preservation Tax Credits in New York: Could Your Hudson Valley Project Qualify?"**
  - High-value magnet for preservation-minded owners; NY State + federal rehabilitation
    tax-credit basics (verify current program details when drafting).

### Pillar E — Sustainable Solutions (the requested focus — a full cluster, and a differentiator)
- **E1. "The Greenest House Is the One Already Standing: Why Restoring Beats Rebuilding"**
  - Angle: embodied carbon — reusing an existing building avoids the carbon of demolition
    + new materials. Ties sustainability directly to his restoration specialty. The most
    on-brand, shareable sustainability piece — lead the cluster with this.
- **E2. "Energy Efficiency in Old Hudson Valley Homes: Comfort and Lower Bills Without Wrecking the Character"**
  - Angle: air-sealing, insulation, and windows done *right* in a historic house — the
    intersection of sustainability and preservation almost no one explains well.
- **E3. "Heat Pumps, Insulation, and What's Actually Worth It in a Hudson Valley Home"**
  - Angle: practical green-upgrade ROI; what pays back and what doesn't; NY incentives
    (NYSERDA / Clean Heat — verify current programs when drafting). Broad search appeal.
- **E4. "Building a Healthy, Low-Energy New Home in the Hudson Valley: A Practical Guide"**
  - Angle: high-performance building basics for new construction — envelope, ventilation,
    electrification — without the jargon.
- **E5. "Reclaimed, Local, and Durable: Sustainable Material Choices for Hudson Valley Building"**
  - Angle: materials that are greener *and* age well; local sourcing; craft.
