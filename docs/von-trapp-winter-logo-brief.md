So the notes give three hard constraints — the mark should be a **letter mark**,

## Design implications (to confirm)

These follow from the requirements above; treat as a starting proposal, not
settled decisions.

### Form
- A **letter mark / monogram** rather than a pictorial or illustrative logo.
  Natural candidates: the initials **VTW** (Von Trapp Winter) or **SW**
  (Severin Winter). Worth exploring a **VTW** monogram as the primary route,
  since that matches the public brand name.
- "Simple" + "distinct" pull in tension: keep the letterforms minimal and
  confident, but find one distinguishing move (a custom ligature, a single
  crossbar/join, or a proportion quirk) so it doesn't read as a default font.
- Given the barn/house restoration trade, a mark with a **built / structural
  feel** (clean geometric letterforms, honest weight, maybe a subtle timber-
  joint or roofline cue) would fit — without becoming a literal house icon.

### Applications (drive the design, not the other way around)
- **Truck sign:** must be legible at distance and from an angle, so favor a
  **single-color, high-contrast** mark with a strong silhouette. Avoid fine
  detail or thin strokes that vanish on a moving vehicle or in vinyl cut.
- **Business card:** "simple, clean" — implies generous whitespace, one accent
  color at most, and the mark working well **small and in one color**.

### Practical deliverables to plan for
- Primary letter mark + a horizontal lockup with the full name "Von Trapp
  Winter" (and optionally "Severin Winter" as the DBA line).
- Single-color and reversed (knockout) versions for vehicle vinyl and print.
- Vector master (SVG) so it scales cleanly from card to truck panel.

## Open questions for Severin
- Which name leads the mark — **Von Trapp Winter (VTW)** or **Severin Winter

- Any color preference, or keep it monochrome for maximum flexibility on the
  truck and card?
- Should the mark hint at the building/restoration craft, or stay purely
  typographic?
