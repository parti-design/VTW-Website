## 1. Growth thesis
Given Severin's wind-down goals — **paid more, work less, less time on site, less
liability** — growth should mean a **steady trickle of high-value, trust-based clients**,
not volume marketing. The engine is **relationships + authority**, not advertising. Fewer,
better engagements; premium positioning; low ongoing effort once primed.

## 2. Target customer segments (ranked)
1. **Out-of-town (mostly NYC) buyers of high-end Hudson Valley homes** — primary. Cash-rich,
   time-poor, don't know local builders, anxious about renovation cost. Largest, best-fit,
   growing pool.
2. **Historic home & barn owners/buyers** — his moat. Smaller pool, almost no real
   competition, high referral velocity (the preservation community talks).
3. **Pre-purchase due-diligence clients** — not a separate segment so much as the *front
   door* to the other two (see entry product).
4. **Architects, designers, estate managers** — clients *and* referrers.

## 3. The entry product (keystone offer)
A **fixed-fee "Pre-Purchase Property Assessment"**: half-day visit + short written report
(condition, buildability, realistic cost range, red flags). Why it fits *him*:
- High $/hour, finite, **no liability tail**, no job-site babysitting.
- Easy "yes" for a buyer about to spend $1M+ — cheap insurance.
- Converts into larger owner's-rep / design engagements (removes the hard first "yes").
- Gives referral partners a concrete, low-stakes thing to recommend.
- Fixed-fee pre-purchase advisory is **standard practice** in this field (see scan).

## 4. Competitive scan — the service is a recognized category
Known as "owner's representative," "homeowner's advocate," or "construction advisor."
Buyers already understand it. Three overlapping flavors:

| Business | Where | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Vigilus Home Support** | Rhinebeck, HV | Closest *local* comp; residential owner's rep + property/rental mgmt (concierge lean). "We're local, even when you're not." |
| **ADVOCATE – Residential Construction Advisors** | National | The fullest model; sells **Pre-Purchase Advisory** as a product. "Your representative, not a vendor." Blueprint to emulate. |
| **Mid Hudson Construction Mgmt / CICC** | HV | Owner's rep / CM, but lean commercial-institutional. |
| **Peak Projects / Gryphon / HCMG** | National | Luxury residential owner's rep; show the premium ceiling. |
| **Olde Mohawk / Clemco / John Canning** | NE / NY | Historic restoration — but they *do the contracting* (conflict) or are architects. |

**Pricing norms:** full owner's rep ≈ **1–5% of project cost** (sliding down as projects
grow); targeted/advisory ≈ **hourly $75–$200+** (Severin's model fits here);
pre-purchase ≈ **fixed fee**.

**Severin's white space:** an *independent, builder-wise owner's advocate with a
historic/barn specialty.* Competitors either bundle construction (conflict of interest),
bundle design (licensing/cost), or are generic commercial PMs. He's the trustworthy
specialist in the middle — and locally, the residential niche is thin.

## 5. Channel map — the incentive insight
Not all referrers are aligned. Separate **conflicted gatekeepers** from **conflict-free
champions**:

| Tier | Channel | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Court actively** | **Buyer's agents**, real-estate attorneys, interior designers, past clients, preservation/land-trust community | Conflict-free or fiduciary-aligned — they *want* to refer |
| **Be the direct destination** | Magazine authority + SEO articles + strong site | Bypasses gatekeepers, compounds over time, multiplies referrals |
| **Selective only** | Collaborative architects (who gladly shed construction admin) | Target the open ones; skip the territorial |
| **Don't bother** | Builders/GCs, **listing agents** | Conflicted — a watchdog/problem-finder threatens their deal |

Key distinctions:
- **Buyer's agent vs. listing agent:** the buyer's agent has a fiduciary duty to the
  buyer and competes on being the trusted local guide — connecting a nervous out-of-town
  buyer to a building expert makes them the hero and de-risks the deal. The **listing
  agent** wants max price + clean close, so problem-finding threatens them. Court buyer's
  agents; avoid listing agents.
- **Architects aren't really competitors** — different job (design vs. management). Many
  residential architects dislike construction admin and cost-enforcement; a rep takes
  that off their plate and protects their reputation. But it's *selective* — collaborative
  yes, ego-driven no.
- **Direct + referral multiply each other:** a referral makes the client Google Severin;
  a strong site + a magazine feature is what converts that into a call.

## 7. How to approach them (referral playbook)
1. **Start warm.** A buyer's agent Severin (or a past client) already knows beats any cold
   approach. List existing contacts first.
2. **Target the individual, not the brokerage.** Pick specific buyer's agents known for
   country homes / historic properties.
3. **Lead with a gift, not a pitch.** Frame the Pre-Purchase Assessment as something *they
   can offer their client*: "I help your out-of-town buyers understand a property before
   they commit — so they buy with confidence and you avoid surprises after closing." It
   de-risks *their* deal and makes them look good.
4. **Make it effortless to refer:** a one-page leave-behind + a clear, memorable line
   ("the builder on the buyer's side of the table").
5. **Reciprocate.** Send them business and leads too — referrals flow back to people who
   give them.
6. **Stay in light, regular contact** — a quarterly coffee, the occasional useful article.
7. **What NOT to do:** don't pitch listing agents or builders (conflicted); don't position
   the assessment as deal-killing — position it as *confidence + negotiation leverage*
   (it usually helps deals close, and gives buyers room to renegotiate).
